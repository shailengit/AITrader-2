Momentum Quality Rotation — Turnover Audit

Audit of the reported 26,891-trade figure, followed by a min_hold_days lever sweep. Period 2020-01-01 → 2026-09-28 · $100,000 capital · generated 2026-09-29

⚠ Read this before the sweep tables

MQR's single-run backtest is path-chaotic, so the return, CAGR, Sharpe and max-drawdown columns in sections 4 and 5 cannot support a decision. Verified 2026-09-30: varying only the transaction cost while holding everything else fixed gives 0 bps → 64.18%, 0.1 bps → 68.61% (+4.4 pts on a 0.06%/yr drag), 0.5 bps → 96.68% (+32.5 pts on a 0.30%/yr drag), 1.0 bps → 56.28%. A repeat of the 5 bps cell reproduced bit-identically, so this is trajectory dependence, not noise.

Mechanism: shares = int(target_value / price) truncates. Fees shrink cash → shrink portfolio_value → shrink target_value → a share count flips → a different entry/exit sequence → the error compounds over 400+ trades and 6.75 years. Trade counts differ per cost level (412/413/407/414/410/408), confirming the paths genuinely diverge.

What still holds: the audit's arithmetic, the exit-reason counts, the turnover rate, and the conclusion that suppressing one exit re-routes churn — all of these are direct structural counts, not returns. What does not: the per-variant return/Sharpe/drawdown figures, and any claim that one bear_exposure level beats another.

1 · Headline

The 26,891 trades were a reporting artifact, not churn.

The sidecar .meta.json stored a batch-wide sum across all 100 runs while every other KPI in the same file was a median. Real per-run turnover is 269 trades (~60 exits per run-year, ~30-day average hold). The defect affected every strategy's sidecar, inflating trade counts by its last batch's run count (2× to 100×). Fixed.

Neither turnover lever works — turnover is load-bearing.

Raising min_hold_days suppressed rotation exits but handed them straight to the trailing stop (total turnover −10%). Arming the trailing stop only after a gain suppressed trailing-stop exits but handed them to rotation and the hard stop (total turnover −14%). Both degraded risk-adjusted performance: max drawdown rose 13–16 points and Sharpe fell on every variant. And the backtest models no transaction costs (fills at next open, no commission or slippage), so reducing exits has no modelled upside — even at 20bps/side a full 14% turnover cut is worth only 0.68%/yr against a 64% CAGR.

What a turnover cut is actually worth

The adapter models no transaction costs — no commission, no slippage. Each round trip trades ~40% of portfolio notional (sell ~1/5, replacement buy ~1/5), so 61.1 exits/yr is ~24.4× portfolio turnover annually. Drag and savings:

Cost per sideAnnual dragSaved by a −14% turnover cut
5 bps1.22%0.17%
10 bps2.44%0.34%
20 bps4.89%0.68%

Against a 64.2% CAGR, the best-case saving is well under 1%/yr — purchased with 13–16 points of extra max drawdown and a lower Sharpe. Not a trade worth making.

6 · Transaction-cost model & bear_exposure decision

A cost model now exists

cost_bps charges the filled notional per side on all three fill paths (main exit, rotation exit, entry), and round-trip P&L includes both fill costs so win rate and profit factor are honest too. Default 0.0 is a verified no-op — the bear_0.50 / cost_0 cell has reproduced 2727.26% identically across four separate processes. 8 unit tests.

Return is noise — a +2.05 median inside a 38-point spread, winning 4 of 6 starts, losing by 17 on one and winning by 21 on another. Drawdown is the only directional effect — worse on 5 of 6 starts, and theory-consistent (more exposure in downtrends means deeper drawdowns). Loosening the filter therefore trades a predicted cost for an unmeasurable gain, with Sharpe flat.

Supporting point for keeping a filter: bear 1.00 (always fully invested) has the lowest median CAGR and lowest Sharpe of the three — worse than 0.50. The regime filter earns its place; loosening it does not.

bear_exposure dose-response — and the final verdict

Since higher exposure made drawdowns consistently worse, the logical next test was lower exposure. Four levels over the same six paired start dates. The 0.50 column reproduced the previous run's values exactly (64.18 / 45.61 / 55.76 / 49.90 / 47.87 / 64.83), confirming harness determinism across five independent processes.

Drawdown is NOT ordered by exposure — and the direction is the opposite of the hypothesis. Full cash has the worst median drawdown (48.6%), 0.35 the best, 0.50 in between. The reason is architectural: bear_exposure is a position-size multiplier on new entries only — it does not force-exit existing holdings. At 0.00 the strategy stops buying in downtrends while its existing positions still ride the decline, so it forgoes the recovery without avoiding the drawdown. It is a poor drawdown control, which matters because drawdown is this strategy's real weakness.

7 · Findings

  • minhold_21 is a wash, not a win. Return is flat (+2.6%) and win rate improves (35.9% → 39.0%), but Sharpe slips (1.35 → 1.34), max drawdown worsens (44.8% → 47.5%), and turnover falls only 9.7%. Not a trade worth making on its own.
  • minhold_28 breaks the strategy. Return falls 65% (2727% → 950%), Sharpe collapses to 0.99, and max drawdown rises to 61.0%. A longer minimum hold forces the strategy to sit in deteriorating positions — it hurts return and risk simultaneously.
  • The activation threshold also fails. It suppresses trailing-stop exits exactly as designed (52.7% → 24.4%), but the churn re-routes to rotation and the hard stop (Stop Loss 5 → 24, roughly 5×), total turnover falls only 14%, and max drawdown rises on every variant (44.8% → 57.4/59.9/60.4%) while Sharpe falls on every variant (1.35 → 1.01/1.12/1.06). arm_at_+10pct looks attractive on headline (+27% return) but buys it with +15 points of drawdown and a worse Sharpe.
  • Turnover is load-bearing, not leakage. Both levers show the same structural result: exits cannot be removed, only re-routed. Suppressing one exit reason inflates another. The rapid rotation appears to be how the strategy captures 3-month momentum — with no modelled costs and 49%/yr alpha against SPY's 15.1%, the exits generate the edge rather than leaking it. Recommendation: stop optimizing turnover.
  • Drawdown is the real weakness. Baseline max drawdown is 44.8% over this window (the 100-run batch median was 36.6%), and it is the metric that makes the strategy hard to hold. Any further work should target drawdown directly — bear_exposure (currently 0.50) is the obvious untested lever.
  • The 20% hard stop is dead at baseline (0.5% of exits) — a 12% trail from peak always fires first, so it only triggers on a ≥20% single-day gap. Note it becomes live once the trail is armed late (5 → 24 exits), which is another reason the activation threshold is not free.

Recommended next step: stop optimizing turnover and attack drawdown instead. Two sweeps now agree that exits can only be re-routed, not removed, and that removing them costs 13–16 points of drawdown. The open lever is bear_exposure (currently 0.50, i.e. half size when SPY < SMA200) — untested on this base. A separate correctness gap worth closing: the adapter models no transaction costs, so every backtest here overstates returns by ~1.2–4.9%/yr depending on real costs.

8 · Methodology & caveats

  • Lever comparison uses one full-period backtest per config. This was discovered mid-analysis to be invalid on this strategy — see the warning at the top of this report. Sections 4–6's return/Sharpe/drawdown columns are within the noise floor of path chaos; only the structural counts (exit reasons, trades per year) are reliable. Any lever decision needs the 100-run randomized batch, compared on medians.
  • The sweep baseline (CAGR 64.2%, Sharpe 1.35, 61.1 exits/yr) differs from the 100-run batch headline (median CAGR 75.3%, Sharpe 1.58) because the batch's runs are 2.4–6.4 years with randomized starts, while this is one 6.75-year window containing 2020's crash, 2022's bear market and 2026. Per-year turnover matches closely (61.1 vs 60.0).
  • MQR was made parameterizable for this sweep (**overrides with pinned defaults and unknown-key rejection); defaults are guarded by test against the verified baseline.
  • No per-trade data was exported by the sweep, so this report contains no equity curve or trade list. All figures above come from adapter summary output.
